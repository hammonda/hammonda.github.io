Inside the simulation

Every visible galaxy star is an orbiting tracer particle. Change the view, let an encounter unfold, or turn up the star count to explore your GPU’s capacity.

The physics

Stars move in one or two softened gravitational potentials. The two galaxy centres attract one another; stars respond to both centres. Fixed-step velocity Verlet integration keeps orbits stable. Spiral structure winds up naturally and close passages can form tidal tails.

This is a restricted gravity model: stars do not attract each other. It omits self-consistent dark matter, gas, collisions, and star formation. It is an interactive visual experiment, not a research-grade N-body calculation. Distance and time use normalised units.

The performance

WebGPU compute is preferred, with WebGL2 transform feedback as a fallback. Initialisation, orbital integration, and rendering all operate on GPU-resident star buffers. Integration steps are batched to minimise memory traffic. Half-float lighting, reduced-resolution bloom, and adaptive rendering control pixel cost.

The star count is exact; 1,600 distant background points are added separately. Changing the star count restarts the scene. Adaptive quality changes resolution, never the number of stars. Achievable frame rate depends on your device. Under heavy load, simulation time slows to preserve the fixed integration step.
