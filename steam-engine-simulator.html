How the simulation works · model, geometry & shortcuts

One mechanism, one set of valve events

A double-acting cylinder receives steam at alternate ends. The D-slide valve exposes each end port either to the surrounding steam chest or to the central exhaust cavity. Between those events, the port is covered. Twin mode adds an identical cylinder with its crank 90° ahead; the large cutaway shows the selected cylinder.

The piston and connecting rod follow exact rigid slider–crank geometry. The valve uses a sinusoidal, ideal eccentric drive. Changing cutoff changes its travel and phase; the valve rod and offset eccentric are schematic equivalents, not a complete Stephenson or Walschaerts gear.

x = r(1 − cos θ) + l − √(l² − r² sin² θ)
q = a sin(θ + α)
τ = [(pH − pa)AH − (pR − pa)AR] dx/dθ

Bore 120 mm · stroke 200 mm · connecting rod 400 mm · piston rod 20 mm · clearance 6% of each end’s swept volume. Drawings are sectional schematics; valve port dimensions and passages are enlarged for clarity. The exhaust crossover is a passage behind the inlet passage.

Thermodynamics & limits

This is an idealized indicator-cycle model. An open port sets the chamber to the relevant reservoir pressure. With both ports shut, trapped steam follows pV1.25 = constant. Release and admission have instantaneous pressure equalization. Work is integrated around both p–V loops; power is work per revolution × rpm / 60.

The regulator sets chest pressure linearly between exhaust and boiler pressure. Boiler pressure, exhaust pressure, and shaft rpm are imposed operating conditions. Negative indicated power means external shaft drive is required. The boiler illustration is a reservoir, with no firing, water-level, or boiler energy dynamics. Flow dots show connections and direction, not fluid particles or mass flow. The condenser is represented by its imposed back pressure.

The model omits steam tables, phase change, condensation, finite port flow, leakage, wall heat transfer, mechanical friction and flywheel acceleration. Performance is an educational estimate of indicated output, not a prediction for a real engine. Shorter cutoff also changes release and compression timing. Head and rod cutoff fractions differ because the connecting rod has finite length.

Efficient by design

Moving parts and steam markers use instanced WebGL 2 geometry in one draw call, with a Canvas 2D fallback. Stationary linework is drawn only when the view changes. A 4,096-step typed-array cycle is rebuilt only when pressure or valve settings change. Frame work reuses buffers; charts and numeric labels update less often than the mechanism. Animation sleeps when paused or the tab is hidden. No downloads, libraries, fonts, or network services are required.

Using the laboratory

Space pauses or resumes. ← / → step the selected crank by 5°; hold Shift for 1°. F fits the whole engine. Use the angle slider or a process button to freeze the mechanism. Drag the diagram to pan; scroll or pinch to zoom. Playback speed changes only how quickly the model is shown. At high playback speeds, regular crank motion can appear to alias.
